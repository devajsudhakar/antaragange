About the Place

A Hill Woven With
Rock and Reverence

Antaragange is a remarkable geological and spiritual landmark in the Kolar district of Karnataka, sitting about 70 kilometres east of Bengaluru on the Kolar–Mulbagal road. The hill rises to approximately 1,226 metres and is defined by an extraordinary landscape of volcanic boulders stacked in dramatic formations — one of the most visually striking terrains in all of peninsular India.

The site is celebrated for its naturally formed lava tube caves — some extending deep into the hill and requiring visitors to crawl through narrow passages in complete darkness. These rare geological formations were created millions of years ago when volcanic lava flows cooled unevenly, leaving hollow tunnels behind.

The name "Antaragange" means Ganges from within — a perennial spring emerges from the hill's interior and feeds a sacred tank beside the ancient Kashivishveshwara temple at the base. This makes the site simultaneously a trekker's adventure and a living pilgrimage centre.

History

Origins of the Sacred Hill

Geological Origin · Millions of Years Ago
Born from Volcanic Activity

The hill and its famous caves were shaped by eruptions that swept through the Deccan Plateau. Lava flows cooled at different rates, leaving behind hollow tubes and the signature boulder formations that give Antaragange its surreal, moonscape character. These lava tubes are among the very few known examples in South India.

Ancient Period
Temple Established, Sacred Spring Discovered

Settlers in the Kolar region discovered a spring welling up from within the hill — an astonishing phenomenon in the otherwise semi-arid plateau. The spring was consecrated as a manifestation of the sacred Ganga. A temple to Lord Shiva as Kashivishveshwara was established here, and pilgrims began arriving from across the region.

Medieval Era
Royal Patronage and Temple Expansion

The Gangas, Cholas, and later Vijayanagara-era chieftains patronised the temple. Stone inscriptions in the area record land grants made to the temple trust. The sacred tank was maintained and the complex expanded, establishing Antaragange as an important pilgrimage site on the eastern Karnataka circuit.

Colonial Era
British Geological Surveys

British-era geological surveys documented the hill's unusual terrain and rare cave systems. The Kolar region was already famous for its gold fields, actively mined under British administration. The hill was noted for its strategic elevated viewpoint across the flat Deccan tableland.

Present Day
Popular Trek and Protected Forest Site

Antaragange is today one of Karnataka's most visited day-trek destinations, drawing thousands from Bengaluru every weekend. Managed jointly by the Kashivishveshwara temple trust and the Karnataka Forest Department, conservation efforts focus on protecting the cave ecosystem and managing the large crowds during the annual Mahashivaratri festival.

Experiences

Things to Do & See

🥾
Summit Trek

A moderately challenging 3–5 km trail navigating boulders and rocky ridgelines to the summit. Sweeping views of the Kolar plateau reward the effort. Start by 6:30 AM to beat heat and crowds.

🕳️
Lava Cave Exploration

Crawl through ancient lava tubes that descend into complete darkness. Some tunnels require belly-crawling. A headlamp is essential. Local guides available at the base for ₹100–150 per group.

🙏
Temple Visit & Sacred Dip

Visit the ancient Kashivishveshwara temple and take a ritual dip in the spring-fed tank. Particularly vibrant on Mondays and during Mahashivaratri when thousands ascend the hill by torchlight.

📸
Boulder Photography

The volcanic boulder fields are a photographer's dream. Golden-hour light raking across textured rock creates dramatic compositions. Both wide-angle and telephoto lenses find strong use cases here.

🌄
Sunrise from the Summit

The summit offers an unobstructed eastern horizon stretching flat for hundreds of kilometres. Arrive before 6 AM for dawn colours transitioning from deep indigo to gold over the plateau.

⛺
Night Camping & Stargazing

Camping on the boulder fields is permitted with prior permission from the Kolar Forest Division. Minimal light pollution makes stargazing outstanding. Bonfires are strictly prohibited.

🦅
Bird Watching

The scrub forests shelter Indian Paradise Flycatcher, Shikra, Oriental White-eye, and various sunbirds. Early morning walks on the lower slopes are best. Carry binoculars.

🌿
Nature Walk & Rock Pools

After monsoon rains, natural rock pools form in boulder depressions — a refreshing sight in otherwise dry terrain. The lower slopes support dry-deciduous scrub flora unique to the Kolar plateau.

Around the Region

Nearby Attractions

≈ 5 km · Kolar Town Centre

Kolaramma Temple

The presiding deity of Kolar town — Goddess Kolaramma, a form of Parvati/Durga — resides in this ancient temple that gives the city its very name. The main shrine traces its origins to the Ganga and Vijayanagara periods, featuring intricate carvings and a striking gopuram. The annual Kolaramma Jatre (chariot festival) draws lakhs of devotees. An absolute must-visit when in Kolar.

≈ 15 km · Kammasandra, Kolar District

Koti Lingeswara Temple

This extraordinary temple complex houses one crore (10 million) Shivalingas — one of the largest concentrations of lingas in India. The lingas are arranged across a vast network of interlocking pools and mandapas. Every devotee undertaking a Kashi Yatra is believed to visit here. The sheer scale and spiritual intensity of the site is unlike anywhere else in Karnataka.

≈ 7 km · Kolar Town

Someshwara Temple

A beautifully preserved 14th-century Vijayanagara-era temple dedicated to Lord Shiva, set beside a grand stepped pushkarini tank. Known for intricate stone carvings on its mandapas and the spectacular Shivaratri Rathotsava chariot procession that draws devotees from across the district every year.

≈ 12 km · Kolar Gold Fields (KGF)

Kolar Gold Fields (KGF)

Once the world's deepest gold mine and British India's most productive mining district. The heritage town retains colonial-era bungalows, St. Mary's Church, and the famous Oorgaum area. Internationally famous after the KGF films. Worth a heritage walk for its unique industrial and colonial history.

≈ 18 km · Kolar District

Bethamangala Lake

A scenic reservoir set amid rocky outcrops outside Kolar. A reliable birding destination from October to February — painted storks, pelicans, open-billed storks, and migratory ducks arrive in large numbers. The boulder-and-water landscape is also excellent for photography.

≈ 65 km · Chikkaballapur District

Nandi Hills

One of Karnataka's most beloved hill stations at 1,478 m. Features Tippu Sultan's summer palace, the ancient Bhoga Nandeeshwara temple complex at the base, and the spectacular sea-of-clouds phenomenon at sunrise. A natural pairing with Antaragange for a two-destination weekend road trip from Bengaluru.

Visitor Information

🌤️

Best Time to Visit

October – February

Post-monsoon and winter bring cool, clear weather (15–25°C). Avoid peak summer (Mar–May). Monsoon adds greenery but trails become slippery.

🕗

Access Hours

5:00 AM – 6:00 PM

The hill is accessible from early morning. Night camping requires advance permission from the Kolar Forest Range Officer. Temple darshan begins at 6 AM.

🎟️

Entry Fee

No Entry Fee

Currently free to visit. Parking ₹20–50. Cave guide ₹100–200 per group. Temple donations are voluntary. Forest camping permit approx. ₹50–100.

🚗

How to Reach

70 km from Bengaluru

Via NH 75 (Old Madras Road) — ~1.5 hr drive. Nearest railway: Kolar (8 km). KSRTC buses to Kolar town; share autos to the trailhead from Kolar bus stand.

Practical Tips for Visitors

  • 💧 Carry 1.5–2 litres of water per person. There is no water source on the trail above the temple tank.
  • 👟 Wear sturdy closed-toe trekking shoes. Sandals and flip-flops are unsafe on the boulder route.
  • 🔦 A headlamp or torch is essential for caves. Phone torches drain fast in deep tunnels and leave you stranded in darkness.
  • ☀️ Apply sunscreen and carry a hat. Rocky terrain reflects heat strongly — very uncomfortable after 10 AM in summer.
  • 📱 Mobile signal is weak or absent on the summit. Download offline maps before you start — Google Maps offline works well.
  • 🧳 Pack light — a 15–20 litre daypack is ideal. No glass bottles; carry snacks in reusable containers.
  • 👶 Suitable for children aged 10 and above. Cave sections require crawling; not recommended for elderly or those with knee problems.
  • 🏥 Nearest hospital: District Hospital, Kolar (~10 km). Carry a basic first-aid kit with bandages and antiseptic.
  • 🚿 Dress modestly at the temple — cover shoulders and knees, remove footwear before the main shrine.

Responsible Travel

Do's & Don'ts

✅ Do's

  • ✔ Start early — by 6–7 AM — to beat the heat, crowds, and get the best light.
  • ✔ Carry all your garbage back down. There are no bins on the trail or summit.
  • ✔ Trek in groups of 2 or more, especially inside caves, and inform someone of your plan.
  • ✔ Use a local guide for cave exploration on your first visit — they know which tunnels connect where.
  • ✔ Respect temple etiquette — cover up, speak softly, remove footwear.
  • ✔ Stick to existing trails to protect scrub vegetation on the slopes.
  • ✔ Save the Forest Range Officer's number offline before you go — no signal at the summit.
  • ✔ Carry insect repellent during monsoon and post-monsoon months.

⛔ Don'ts

  • ✗ Do not enter deep cave tunnels alone or without a working torch.
  • ✗ Do not litter — no plastic, food waste, or temple offerings scattered on the hill.
  • ✗ Do not play loud music — it disturbs wildlife and other visitors.
  • ✗ Do not pluck plants, disturb nesting birds, or harm bats in the caves.
  • ✗ Do not light bonfires — dry vegetation is highly fire-prone, especially in summer.
  • ✗ Do not trek in heavy rain — volcanic boulders become dangerously slippery when wet.
  • ✗ Do not carve names or paint on rocks — these boulders are millions of years old.
  • ✗ Do not bring glass bottles or alcohol onto the hill — it is a protected forest area.
